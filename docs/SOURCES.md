# Sources

What Brume draws on: the research and guidelines we read, the open-source projects we studied, the standards we follow, and where the design comes from. This is a list of reading, not a claim that Brume does what any study found.

**How we read.** We worked from abstracts, search summaries, official pages and guideline summaries. We did not read the full text of the papers below. Items marked **‡** we knew only from a secondary source (a press release, a news piece, a review or a summary page), not even from the abstract. **[industry]** marks studies run or funded by the company that sells the method, or whose authors hold its patents.

## 2. Open-source projects

We took **no code** from any of these. We read them for published parameters and ideas, and wrote Brume's tone pattern ourselves (`core/.../sound/Tones.kt`). None of them is evidence that the method works.

| Project | Licence | What we learned |
|---|---|---|
| [headphonejames/acrn](https://github.com/headphonejames/acrn) | GPL-3.0 | The most used implementation. Four tones per 1.5 Hz cycle in a fresh random order, three cycles on and two off; each tone sounds for the first half of its slot. Brume's gate follows it. |
| [headphonejames/acrn-react](https://github.com/headphonejames/acrn-react) | GPL-3.0 | The same pattern in a newer interface; a cross-check of the numbers. |
| [r4ai/tinnitus-reducer-acrn](https://github.com/r4ai/tinnitus-reducer-acrn) | MIT | The same tone formula and timing, in Svelte; per-tone level trims so the four sound equally loud. |
| [swaghax/ACRN](https://github.com/swaghax/ACRN) | none stated | The same formula in Python, with 50 ms fades on each tone. |
| [LukeusMaximus/acrn-tinnitus-treatment](https://github.com/LukeusMaximus/acrn-tinnitus-treatment) | MIT | A literal reading of the paper's wording: tones log-equidistant between half and twice the pitch. Brume does not offer it. |
| [Aaronminer1/tinnitus-suite](https://github.com/Aaronminer1/tinnitus-suite) | none stated | Ideas only: log-sweep pitch matching, checking consistency across sessions in cents, care with octave confusion, a long fade-out. |
| TinnitusTalk DIY thread on acoustic CR | forum post | The community formula for the four tones, said to be fitted to Desyncra's device: floor(0.773f − 44.5), floor(0.903f − 21.5), floor(1.09f + 52), floor(1.395f + 26.5). (No link in our notes.) |

The outer two tones agree with the 0.766× to 1.4× bounds reported for Tass et al. 2012. The middle two are community-derived and **not verified** against the paper; nor are tone duration, ramps, level or daily time. The 25 ms raised-cosine edges are our own choice. Acoustic CR is patented (Tass, licensed to Desyncra/ANM).

## 3. Standards and guidance

- **WHO–ITU H.870** (2018, revised 2022), *Guidelines for safe listening devices/systems*: a weekly dose of 80 dBA for 40 hours, 75 dBA in sensitive mode, every +3 dB halving the time. <https://itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-t/rec/h/T-REC-H.870-202203-I!!TOC-HTM-E.htm>
- **NIOSH Sound Level Meter app** (CDC, 2017) and NIOSH's tests of smartphone sound-meter accuracy, for what a phone can and can't measure. ‡
- **Android sound dose** (AOSP, source.android.com), headphone dose over a rolling 7 days; makers may change it.
- **France, décret n° 2017-1244**: music venues capped at 102 dB(A) over 15 minutes. ‡
- **EU MDR, Regulation (EU) 2017/745**, and **MDCG 2019-11** (revised June 2025) on software qualification: a product's intended purpose, what it claims, decides whether it is a medical device. This is why Brume makes no treatment claims anywhere. <https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11>
- **GDPR, Articles 9 and 89**: journal entries are health data, which is one reason they never leave the phone unless you export them. <https://gdpr-text.com/read/article-89/>
- **Patient organisations**: Tinnitus UK (<https://tinnitus.org.uk/>), France Acouphènes, and France's AFREPA network of multidisciplinary teams.

## 4. Design

- **Breathly** ([mmazzarolo/breathly-app](https://github.com/mmazzarolo/breathly-app)): the approach of a full-screen view while something plays, one calm field with what is playing in words, the timer, a pause and a way back. We took the approach, not its look; Brume draws its own drifting horizon lines.
- **Material 3** (<https://m3.material.io>), through **Gamme** ([kud/gamme](https://github.com/kud/gamme), MIT), the shared design system for these Android apps: components, type scale and the rule that nothing rests on colour alone.

## 5. A note

Brume is made by someone who lives with tinnitus, not by a doctor. It draws on the published research and clinical guidance above, read as carefully as we could, to offer some calm to anyone who wants to relax. It isn't a treatment or a medical device, and it doesn't replace a doctor, an ENT or an audiologist. If something here looks wrong or out of date, please open an issue.
